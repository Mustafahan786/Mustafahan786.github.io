Current research GIST · September 2025–present

Model-Based Digital PI Control for High-Speed Contact-Mode AFM

This project investigates Z-axis tracking in contact-mode AFM using an identified plant model and a digital PI controller. Imaging consistency is evaluated by comparing trace and retrace topographies at increasing scan frequencies.

My contributionExperimental system identification, PI controller design and STM32 implementation, analogue signal-conditioning revisions, and imaging experiments.

Key resultAt 2 and 5 Hz, trace–retrace agreement was high (r ≥ 0.977; NRMSE ≤ 7.7%). Agreement decreased at 10 and 20 Hz.

Course project GIST · Winter 2025–26

Voice-Coil Stage Design and Feedback Control

I designed and built a single-axis voice-coil positioning stage to investigate feedback control. The work combined PA51 current-drive characterisation, laser-based position sensing, measured frequency responses and evaluation of analogue compensators.

My contributionDesigned and assembled the stage, configured the PA51 current drive and laser position feedback, measured frequency responses, and evaluated analogue compensation.

Key resultIn the bench tests, the PI-controlled response drifted and did not settle at the commanded set-point. PD and lead compensation produced more stable tracking under the tested conditions.

Research project NUST · June 2022–August 2023

Real-Time sEMG Gesture Control for Upper-Limb Rehabilitation

This project developed an sEMG-driven video-game protocol for interactive post-stroke hand rehabilitation. Gradient-boosting gesture recognition enabled real-time control, while Fitts’ law and task-specific performance metrics assessed user performance across repeated sessions.

My contributionDeveloped the sEMG processing pipeline and candidate classifiers, selected and deployed gradient boosting, and co-developed the task interfaces and evaluation protocol.

Key resultGradient boosting enabled real-time task control. Space Shooter completion times followed task difficulty, supporting performance assessment using Fitts’ law.

Final-year project REHABOTICS · NUST · 2023–2024

sEMG-Controlled Soft Robotics Glove for Game-Based Hand Rehabilitation

Integrated machine-learning-based sEMG gesture recognition with a soft pneumatic glove and a one-degree-of-freedom rehabilitation game, combining visual feedback with assisted hand movement.

My contributionSoft actuator design, ANSYS simulation and fabrication, with contributions to machine-learning gesture recognition and integrated glove–game control.

Key resultAchieved approximately 120–180° of actuator bending at 100 kPa and integrated gesture-controlled glove assistance with an interactive rehabilitation game.
